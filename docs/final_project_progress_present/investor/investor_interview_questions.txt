INVESTOR INTERVIEW QUESTIONS — Part 2 of Final Project Video
=============================================================

Framing
-------
The teammate's earlier interview covered a TECHNICAL / EXPERT angle:
  (1) Biggest challenges in cybersecurity
  (2) Is the increasing use of AI in cybersecurity an issue
  (3) Limitations of modern IDS
  (4) Advantages of hardware-accelerated ML for IDS

These are "is the technology needed?" questions. Good, but not what Part 2
needs. Part 2 must convince viewers (the audience IS investors in the
framing of this presentation) that there is *real money to be made* in
hardware-accelerated ML IDS. So this second interview should answer the
investor's four gut questions:

  A. Is the market big and growing?                  (TAM / trajectory)
  B. Will real customers pay real money for this?    (WTP / pain urgency)
  C. Can a startup actually win versus incumbents?   (moat / "why us")
  D. Is there a credible exit?                       (acquirer landscape)

Interviewee is framed as "an investor" — i.e. someone who can speak to the
commercial / market / venture lens (VC, angel, finance contact, corp-dev,
industry analyst, founder who has raised money, or a parent's friend
playing that role per Prof Cho's explicit encouragement).

Target output: ~20 seconds of quotable clip for the 90–120 sec video.
Interview length: 10–15 minutes. Pick 5–7 of the 8 below based on time.
Each question has an INTENT note so it is obvious why it is on the list
and what kind of quote to cut for.


Priority Questions (zero overlap with the teammate's interview)
---------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. MARKET SIZE & TRAJECTORY
    "When you look at the market for network security hardware today —
    SmartNICs, inspection appliances, security-focused DPUs — how big do
    you think that market actually is, and what growth rate would get you
    excited enough to back a startup in the space?"

    INTENT: Get a number on camera. Even a rough one ("tens of billions,"
    "20-plus percent CAGR") is gold. Answers category A.

Q2. WILLINGNESS TO PAY / BUYER ROI
    "Put yourself in the seat of a CISO or infrastructure buyer. What
    argument do they actually need to hear to approve a new piece of
    security hardware — avoided breach cost, a compliance checkbox,
    performance headroom, something else? Where does the dollar justify
    itself?"

    INTENT: Pull-quote on how real buyers rationalize spend. Answers B.
    The "avoided breach cost" framing is the cleanest for the video.

Q3. FIRST-REVENUE CUSTOMER
    "If a startup like this had a working prototype tomorrow, which buyer
    archetype do you think is the fastest first revenue — hyperscalers,
    telcos, financial services, federal, healthcare, or enterprise data
    centers? Who is the easiest door to open, and who eventually writes
    the biggest checks?"

    INTENT: Concrete go-to-market signal. A name-drop ("banks will buy
    this first") is a great soundbite. Answers B.

Q4. COMPETITIVE MOAT vs. INCUMBENTS
    "NVIDIA BlueField, AMD Pensando, and Intel IPU already ship
    programmable SmartNICs. What's the honest case for why a startup
    building a custom ML-IDS chip can win here — instead of being
    steamrolled by the incumbents' distribution and customer lists?"

    INTENT: This is the question investors always ask. A good answer
    either validates a moat (specialization, toolchain, ML models,
    footprint/cost, OEM design-wins) or gives us homework. Answers C.

Q5. WHY NOW — THE TIMING WINDOW
    "From a pure investor lens, why is 2026 a better moment to back
    hardware-accelerated ML network security than, say, 2020? What has
    changed — AI adoption, threat landscape, regulation, silicon economics
    — that makes this a live opportunity right now?"

    INTENT: Every investor pitch has a "why now." A punchy answer here
    is the setup for our market-growth slide. Answers A + C.

Q6. REGULATORY / COMPLIANCE TAILWINDS
    "Which regulations or compliance regimes do you see actually moving
    security budgets in 2026 — PCI-DSS, HIPAA, CMMC, EU AI Act, SEC
    cyber-disclosure rules? Are any of them strong enough on their own
    to force a purchase cycle we could ride?"

    INTENT: Compliance = forced spend = predictable revenue. Investors
    love this. A name-drop of a specific regulation makes our market
    claim look researched. Answers A + B.

Q7. EXIT LANDSCAPE
    "When strategics like Broadcom, Cisco, Palo Alto, AMD, or NVIDIA look
    at a company like this, what do they actually pay for — the silicon
    IP, the ML models, the customer base, the team? And what does a
    realistic exit path in this category look like today?"

    INTENT: Answers D directly. A quote naming a realistic acquirer or
    "nine-figure exits are normal in this category" would land perfectly.

Q8. THE PASS / LEAN-IN TEST   *** money-question candidate ***
    "If this company pitched you tomorrow, what is the ONE thing that
    would make you pass, and what is the ONE piece of evidence that would
    make you lean in and want to invest?"

    INTENT: Forces a direct, quotable judgment. The "lean-in" answer is
    the single most powerful soundbite we can get — it's an investor
    effectively endorsing the direction on camera. Pair a yes-leaning
    answer here with Q1's number for a killer 20-sec cut.


Ranking if Time Runs Short
--------------------------
  Must-ask:    Q1 (market size), Q2 (WTP), Q8 (lean-in)
  Strong-add:  Q4 (moat), Q7 (exit)
  Nice-to-add: Q3 (first buyer), Q5 (why now), Q6 (regulation)


Clip-Selection Shortlist (what to listen for during edit)
---------------------------------------------------------
  * A DOLLAR AMOUNT or GROWTH RATE           -> use for Hook (0-15s)
  * A NAMED VERTICAL that will buy first     -> use for Market framing
  * A NAMED ACQUIRER / EXIT                  -> use for Close
  * A DIRECT "I'd lean in" quote             -> use as the pull-quote
  * A NAMED REGULATION forcing spend         -> use for Market framing


Interviewee-Specific Tweaks
---------------------------
If the interviewee is NOT a literal VC (e.g. a family friend in finance
or tech), soften any question that puts them on the spot:

  Q8 alternate: "If you were mentoring a founder with this idea, what
                 would you tell them to prove before raising money?"

  Q7 alternate: "If a bigger company bought a startup like this, what do
                 you think they'd be buying — the chip, the software, or
                 the people?"

Same signal, less awkward. Professor's humble-framing rule applies here
too — don't let the interviewee over-claim on our behalf.


Ground Rules (keep on camera truthful)
--------------------------------------
  * Do not coach the interviewee to endorse specific performance numbers
    (e.g. "say we're 3.28x faster"). Our 3.28x is vs our own scalar
    baseline only — not vs commercial silicon. Over-claiming on camera
    will backfire with a technical audience.
  * Get written recording + publishing consent before the call.
  * Send the final cut to the interviewee before the video is posted.
  * If a quote gets cut for the video, trim for honesty — no clip that
    materially misrepresents what they said.
