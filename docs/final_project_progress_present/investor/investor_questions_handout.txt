Investor Interview — Questions (sent to you in advance so you can think
them over before we talk)
=========================================================================

Thanks for agreeing to sit for a short interview for my final project.

Quick context
-------------
* I'm building a hardware-accelerated machine-learning intrusion detection
  system — basically a network card (FPGA-based SmartNIC) that classifies
  traffic with a small neural network on-chip instead of sending every
  packet to a CPU or GPU server.
* The final deliverable is a short video (90–120 seconds total), split
  into three audience-specific segments. The part you're helping with is
  aimed at investors / commercial reviewers and has to answer one question:
  "is there a real market here, or is this just a cool demo?"
* I'll probably ask 5–7 of the questions below over ~10–15 minutes. I'll
  use a ~60 second clip from the recording in the final video.
* Please confirm at the start of the call that you're OK with me
  recording and publishing (only in the class) a short clip. I'll send you the final cut
  before anything goes public.
* Feel free to speak plainly. Skepticism is more useful than polite endorsement — the
  goal is an honest take, not a commercial.


Main questions (5)
------------------

1. How big do you think this market is today, in dollars? Billions?
   Tens of billions?

2. If a startup had this working tomorrow, who would buy it first —
   banks, telcos, cloud providers, government, or enterprise?

3. NVIDIA BlueField and AMD Pensando already ship programmable
   SmartNICs. Can a startup win against them, yes or no?

4. What budget would a CISO realistically approve for one of these
   security boxes — $10K, $100K, $1M+?

5. If this company pitched you tomorrow, would you want to invest?
   Yes or no, and why?


Possible follow-ups (only if we have time)
------------------------------------------

6. Is any current regulation — PCI-DSS, HIPAA, CMMC, EU AI Act, SEC
   cyber-disclosure — strong enough on its own to force a purchase
   cycle we could ride?

7. Why 2026 and not 2020? What's changed to make this a live
   opportunity now?

8. When a strategic like Broadcom, Cisco, or NVIDIA acquires a
   company like this, what are they actually paying for — the chip,
   the software, the customer base, or the team?

9. If this product existed and worked today, would you deploy it in
   your own network? Yes or no?

10. Put yourself in a security buyer's seat. What argument actually
    makes the dollar justify itself — avoided breach cost, compliance,
    performance headroom, something else?


No prep required
----------------
You don't need slides, numbers, or homework. A direct gut answer on
each question is exactly what works best on video. If any question
doesn't apply to your experience, just say so and we'll skip it.

Happy to share more about the underlying technology if it would help
you answer any of these more concretely — just ask.
