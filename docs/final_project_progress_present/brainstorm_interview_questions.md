# Brainstorm — Investor Interview Question Bank

For recording the "investor" interview clip for Part 2. Use parents' friends or professional contacts playing the investor role (per professor's explicit suggestion). Goal: extract 10-20 seconds of clip that supports the market thesis.

## 1. Pre-Interview Prep

Before the call:
1. **Brief the interviewee** (5-min email or call): "*I'm making a project video for a class. I want to get your reaction to a new technology I built — an AI-powered network security chip. You don't need to prepare; I'll walk you through it and ask your opinion.*"
2. **Set expectations**: "*We'll talk for ~15 minutes; I'll only use ~20 seconds in the final video. If you say something I'd like to include, I'll confirm with you before publishing.*"
3. **Confirm recording consent** in writing (email is fine): "*OK for me to record this Zoom call and use an excerpt in my class project video?*"
4. **Choose recording mode**: Zoom (auto-records), Google Meet (via the Record feature), or in-person (phone + lapel mic).

**[DIG DEEPER]:** Send a one-page project summary ahead of time? Pros: better-informed answers. Cons: answers feel less spontaneous. Probably skip unless the interviewee is technical.

## 2. Interview Flow (~15 min)

### 2.1 Warm-up (~2 min)
- "*Thanks for taking the time. Can you tell me a bit about what you do?*"
- Icebreaker; gets them comfortable on camera.

### 2.2 Explain the project (~3 min, YOU talking)
Plain-language pitch:
> "*I'm building a tiny computer chip that sits inside a network device — like the router in your home, or the servers at a hospital. The chip watches network traffic and uses AI to recognize attacks in real time. Think of it as a little guard that knows the difference between your grandson's FaceTime call and a scammer dialing from overseas.*"

Adapt depending on interviewee's technical depth. Prepare a 30-sec version and a 90-sec version; read the room.

### 2.3 Market validation questions (~4 min)

Pick 3-4:

1. **"In your industry, what are people spending money on to protect themselves from digital threats?"**
   - Surfaces the budget size, who buys, what categories.
2. **"Is cybersecurity a growing concern at work — or stable, or shrinking?"**
   - Gets growth-rate language.
3. **"Have you heard of any incidents in your industry where something like this would have helped?"**
   - Real-world story hook (gold for the video).
4. **"If I told you there's a chip that runs AI-powered security detection on the device itself — no cloud — would that interest you? Why or why not?"**
   - Key positioning question.
5. **"Do you know anyone at your company who'd want to pilot something like this?"**
   - Soft-lead generation; shows real interest.

### 2.4 Differentiation questions (~3 min)

1. **"The big names in cybersecurity right now are CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Fortinet. Do you feel like those cover everything? Or are there gaps?"**
2. **"What's one thing existing solutions DON'T do that you wish they did?"**
3. **"If you were investing, would you back a team building new hardware for this, or would you stick to software?"**

### 2.5 The money question (~2 min)

**Hard question**: "*Would you fund this, or back a company like this, if you saw it on a pitch deck?*"

Follow-up if yes: "*What would you want to see first to feel confident?*"
Follow-up if no: "*What would we have to change to flip you?*"

**[DIG DEEPER]:** "Would you fund this" puts the interviewee on the spot. Consider softer: "*If you were mentoring a founder with this idea, what would you tell them?*" — same signal, less awkward.

### 2.6 Closing (~1 min)

1. "*Is there anyone you think I should talk to next?*" (Referral — bonus interview.)
2. "*Can I send you the final video when it's done?*" (Warm-fuzzy close.)
3. "*Thanks again. I'll send you a clip of anything I'm planning to use before I publish.*"

## 3. What to Actually Cut into the Video

Look for clips where the interviewee says something that:
- **Names a real dollar amount** or growth number ("*Our company spent $X on security last year.*")
- **Expresses an unmet need** ("*Nothing I've seen does this on-device.*")
- **Endorses the direction** ("*Every data center will need this eventually.*")
- **Admits fear** ("*We had a scare last year where...*")

Avoid clips where:
- They use industry-specific jargon that a progress-lecture audience won't parse.
- They sound unsure ("*Well, um, maybe, I don't know.*")
- They contradict your thesis (if you disagree with their take, don't use it; use a different interviewee).

**[DIG DEEPER]:** Edit for honesty. Don't cherry-pick a clip that materially misrepresents what they said. Prof Cho's humble-framing principle applies here too.

## 5. Logistics Checklist

- [ ] Decide recording mode (Zoom / in-person / phone)
- [ ] Test equipment beforehand (5-min dry run with a family member)
- [ ] Have transcription plan (Otter.ai, Zoom's built-in, YouTube auto-captions)
- [ ] Backup recording method (phone recording as a second track — cheap insurance)
- [ ] Quiet room booked for the interview
- [ ] Written consent captured
- [ ] Time-zone confirmed if interviewee is remote
- [ ] Send calendar invite with Zoom link at least 24 hours in advance

**[DIG DEEPER]:** Assign a fallback interviewee. If the primary cancels 24 hours before, can the backup fill in on short notice?

## 6. Post-Interview Workflow

1. **Within 24 hours**: send thank-you email, confirm clip-usage consent in writing.
2. **Within 48 hours**: transcribe the interview, mark the "usable" 30-sec chunks.
3. **Within 72 hours**: edit the clip into a rough cut of the investor video.
4. **Before publishing**: send the final clip to the interviewee for approval.

**[DIG DEEPER]:** If the interviewee is uncomfortable with the final clip, re-edit or drop them. Preserving the relationship > using the clip. You may need them again for May 13 or future projects.

## 7. Sample Question Variants (for different interviewee types)

### 7.1 If interviewee is in finance / investing
- "*What's your CISO reporting as the top threats for 2026?*"
- "*Would your portfolio companies want something like this?*"

### 7.2 If interviewee is in healthcare
- "*HIPAA keeps coming up in privacy conversations. Does this change your buying criteria?*"
- "*When was your hospital / practice last breached, if ever?*"

### 7.3 If interviewee is in tech / product
- "*You've worked with AWS / Azure / GCP. Where's the gap for on-device security?*"
- "*If you could drop one piece of cloud infrastructure and replace it with hardware, what would it be?*"

### 7.4 If interviewee is non-technical (uncle, family friend)
- "*Have you ever been scammed online? What would you pay to prevent that?*"
- "*If your bank said 'we have a new chip that protects you,' would you trust them more?*"

**[DIG DEEPER]:** Customize 3-5 questions per interviewee before the call. Generic questions get generic answers.
